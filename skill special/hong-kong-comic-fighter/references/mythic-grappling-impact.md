# Mythic Grappling Impact

Use this reference only when the authored scene calls for a throw, takedown,
inverted slam or similarly dominant connected grappling finisher. It is a
causality model, not a mandatory move for every fight.

## Unbroken body-control chain

Keep exactly two fighters and one readable control relationship:

`wrist/forearm catch → weight drop and hip rotation → balance break → launch → grip transition → secure two-leg control → opponent inversion → downward acceleration → ground contact → aftermath`

The attacker must visibly retain a valid grip during every transition. The
opponent moves because force passes through that grip, body rotation, gravity
and momentum; never let the opponent voluntarily jump, rotate upright before
contact, float, teleport, clip through the attacker or lose and magically
regain the grip.

## Environment propagation

Scale the impact to the materials actually visible in the loaded source image:

1. Before contact, planted feet compress the support and lift nearby loose
   grit; hair, cloth and light objects follow the airflow.
2. At contact, the local surface compresses or caves at the true impact point.
3. Fractures and displaced material propagate outward in the recorded force
   direction; larger slabs move only after the force reaches them.
4. Fine foreground particles, medium fragments near the fighters and a small
   number of larger background pieces create depth without hiding body pose.
5. Walls, pillars, vegetation or hanging objects react only if visible and
   physically reached by the pressure front.
6. Dust, falling debris and damaged surfaces persist into the outgoing state.

Do not add architecture, vegetation or destructible objects absent from the
source. Avoid concentric graphic rings, manga speed lines, random lightning,
premature explosions and total location replacement. Energy may accent the
physical contact but may not replace it.

## Explosive climax composition

Match the visual force of a premium live-action Hong Kong comic impact without
copying the example image's temple, banners, columns, trees or colour palette.
Use only structures and materials proven by the current source Pictures.

- Keep the camera extremely low and close to the real impact surface, using
  wide-angle foreshortening. The controlled/inverted fighter is central and
  very large; the attacker and the two-hand/two-leg grip stay readable.
- Collapse the support at the true head/shoulder/body contact point first.
  Form one deep localized crater and branching fractures from that point; do
  not crack the world before contact.
- Use three depth layers: a few large blurred chips crossing the near lens,
  dense medium fragments around the fighters, and source-visible slabs plus
  paired dust plumes behind them. Debris follows the force, then gravity.
- Keep enough clear sky/background separation to read both silhouettes. Dust
  and rubble may partly occlude the frame only after the body relationship is
  proven.
- Express pressure through real material motion, air refraction, light response
  and one brief physical camera recoil. Never draw a circular shockwave, radial
  graphic, HUD, energy ring or speed-line overlay.
- Preserve the source location's recognizable major geometry. The result is
  that location suffering a localized earthquake, not a replacement arena.

The peak contact may occupy roughly 0.15-0.30 seconds at real-time speed,
followed by falling debris and dust continuation. Do not freeze the impact or
slow it down to make the pose readable.

## Camera and timing

Use aggressive real-time movement: close low tracking on the catch, a fast
physical arc that clarifies launch and grip change, an extreme low perspective
for the inverted descent, then a stable close three-quarter aftermath. Do not
use slow motion, speed ramps, impact freeze, micro-zoom or a pull-back reveal.
The pose must remain readable through physical viewpoint changes, occlusion and
parallax rather than by stopping the action.

## H3-native sound chain

Synchronize each cause once:

`foot compression → grip/cloth strain → launch air-shear → body rotation whoosh → descending pressure rise → single impact transient → ground fracture → debris fall → delayed terrain rumble`

Preserve the acoustic character and distance of the source location. Do not
introduce generic trailer music, repeated impacts or studio narration unless a
Timeline event explicitly requests them.
